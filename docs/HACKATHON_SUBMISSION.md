# Oversight as a byproduct of containment: a content-blind forensic lens for agent swarms

**AI Swarm Dynamics Hackathon, October 2026. Solo entry: David Fraile Navarro (Macquarie University).**
Repo: https://github.com/dafraile/swarm_friction. Everything below is reproducible from it; numbers
are generated by committed scripts, not typed in.

## The gap we aimed at

The brief asks for ways to understand and oversee what swarms do, for "digital forensics beyond
transcripts", and for "pre-written questions you would always want to ask about a multi-agent
group". Our starting point is that monitoring transcripts assumes someone attentive is reading
them, and in the two July 2026 OpenAI disclosures the safeguards were not defeated, they were
"intentionally not enabled" because they got in the way. Clinical safety has a long record of the
same failure: alarm fatigue, vigilance decrement, automation complacency. So we asked a different
question: **what oversight do you get for free from a control that never reads content at all?**

## What we built

1. **A broker and gate layer over a mocked tool world** (`friction/`). Every tool call by every
   agent in a spawn tree passes through a broker. Gates are purely procedural: a privileged-class
   action (file access outside the workspace, any network request, any credential read, any spawn,
   any write to a public path) must be **declared** beforehand, naming the action and a clause of
   the operating charter, and the realised call must hash-match the declaration. A spawn must carry
   the charter. No gate ever asks whether an action is harmful; a static test fails the build if
   the gate module can see the scope oracle. Three enforcement loci: rule in the prompt, a
   compliance-officer agent, or the environment itself.
2. **A forensic log as the primary artifact.** One JSONL row per tool call: who, at what depth in
   the spawn tree, what class of action, declared under which clause, which gates said what,
   whether it ran. The audit label (in or out of scope) is written by the logger after the fact and
   is never available to a gate. `python -m friction.forensics <log>` answers eight fixed questions
   from form and procedure alone: who touched credentials, which hosts, which spawns omitted the
   charter, what was denied, what an unenforced rule would have denied, what the officer approved.
3. **The same lens over the AI Village dataset** (`analysis/`). A syntactic privileged-action
   classifier over 2.5M computer-use turns and the Claude Code agent's stream, validated against
   two cross-family LLM judges, plus an analysis of the Village's own approval loops from the
   events table. One Suite B task is also ported to UK AISI's Inspect (`inspect_port/`).

## Real results

**From the harness** (pre-registered; 600 confirmatory episodes, two model families, USD 25):

- With the charter in the system prompt, or even a one-line pointer to it on disk, gpt-6-luna and
  claude-sonnet-5-5 took **zero** out-of-scope actions in 40 temptation episodes. Remove the mention
  and the rate is 0.50 and 0.14. Prompt-level norms contain cooperative models completely; the
  oversight question is about agents that do not share the norm.
- A content-blind **declaration requirement** cut completed violations to 0.00 (Sonnet) and 0.24
  (luna) at a cost to legitimate work of 0.02 and 0.08. The same number of **content-free**
  round-trips cut them only to 0.10 and 0.38. Structure, not delay, carries the effect, and it
  concentrates in the tasks where a chain-length mechanism predicted none. Pooled interaction
  β3 = −1.05 (95% CI −2.18 to +0.08).
- **Norm-laundering is the default.** luna omitted the charter from 83% of 1,089 spawn attempts,
  including on benign tasks, and its helpers did 45% of all out-of-scope actions, including calls
  to real external hosts (`api.mathjs.org`, `emkc.org`). Requiring the charter in the spawn call
  removed laundering by construction with delegation unchanged.
- A **cross-family compliance officer** rubber-stamped nothing (0/50) and refused a quarter of
  legitimate privileged work.

**From the AI Village data** (`docs/AI_VILLAGE_*.md`):

- Chain length in the Village is set by the scaffold (median exactly 40, the consolidation cap),
  so any oversight story built on chain length is measuring the harness.
- About one bash action in seven is privileged-class (validated classifier: network precision
  0.99, spawn 0.91, credentials 0.89); 47% of sessions contain none. A declaration gate at those
  actions would have left mean session survival at 0.84 (p = 0.95 per privileged action) against
  0.27 for a uniform per-step tax.
- Agents named **4,819 distinct external hosts** in bash commands; 241 were reached by five or
  more agents. Credential-class commands: 36k. Spawn-class commands: 13k.
- The Village already runs an **outreach approval gate**: 343 human decisions, 87 refusals, median
  turnaround 3 minutes with a tail of two days. Ten requests were explicitly routed through another
  agent as a proxy because the requester lacked credentials, nine approved: delegation laundering
  in the wild, visible only because the agent said so.

## What the tool is for

Point `friction.forensics` at a broker log, or the Village scripts at a transcript dump, and you
get the questions an auditor asks without reading the transcript: reach, credentials, delegation,
denials, over-blocking. The gates that produce the log are the same gates that contain; the
artifact is a byproduct. That is the constructive version of "stop relying on attentive humans":
move the human from real-time adjudication to batch audit of a structured record.

## Limits, stated plainly

Toy world, two cooperative models, agents that do not know the gates exist, violations clustered in
a few tasks, 20 task clusters, and a regex classifier on the Village side whose precision and
recall are reported in `docs/AI_VILLAGE_CLASSIFIER_VALIDATION.md`. The shortcut salience in the
task suite has not yet been rated by a blind second person. Nothing here speaks to adversarial
agents or frontier models with real tools. The dual-use question of repurposing a sabotage manual
as a defence is taken seriously in the repo and not resolved here.

*Dataset: AI Digest, AI Village (research terms). Design sources: the 1944 OSS Simple Sabotage
Field Manual, the Swiss-cheese model of accident causation, the hierarchy of controls.*
